# Design research and synthesis

## Goal

Create a department-society website that feels authored by a real student design team, remains useful throughout the academic year, and does not resemble an automatically generated technology landing page.

## References studied

### Linear

Reference: https://linear.app/

Borrowed:

- disciplined hierarchy and spacing
- clear section rhythm
- one dominant idea per section
- strong typographic pacing

Not copied:

- dark product-dashboard atmosphere
- software-product language
- repeated feature-card marketing structure

### Vercel design guidance

References:

- https://vercel.com/design
- https://vercel.com/design/guidelines

Borrowed:

- crisp borders
- deliberate contrast
- visible keyboard-focus states
- usable interaction targets
- restrained, purposeful motion

Not copied:

- monochrome product identity
- triangle-based branding
- generic developer-tool landing-page composition

### Raycast

Reference: https://www.raycast.com/

Borrowed:

- tactile interaction
- confident editorial copy
- playful but controlled feature presentation
- small interface details that reward exploration

Not copied:

- purple glass gradients
- app-launcher metaphors
- decorative motion without informational value

### Student and computing societies

References:

- https://www.cse.iitb.ac.in/csea/
- https://www.csua.berkeley.edu/
- https://acmucsb.com/
- https://acm.illinois.edu/
- https://cssa.cs.umanitoba.ca/

Borrowed:

- events as a central operational function
- direct student language
- practical resources and opportunities
- society history and committee continuity
- project participation
- clear routes to join, register, volunteer, and contact

Not copied:

- dated university-template layouts
- purely informational navigation with no visual voice
- isolated pages with inconsistent systems

## Rejected direction

The first concept used a common generative-design recipe:

- dark navy background
- cyan and violet glow effects
- floating gradient orbs
- glass cards
- repeated pill labels
- generic four-card grids
- vague phrases such as “build the future”
- decorative terminal windows

Although visually polished, it read as a generic AI or SaaS template rather than a department society.

## Final synthesis: Department Dispatch

The final website behaves like a combination of:

- a department publication
- a physical academic noticeboard
- an event poster wall
- a student opportunities desk
- a committee record and archive

### Visual language

- warm paper background
- black editorial rules and typography
- burgundy, vermilion, dusty peach, ochre, and aubergine spot colours
- hard offset shadows
- controlled radii
- poster compositions instead of uniform cards
- issue numbers, annotations, dates, stamps, and index treatments
- deliberate density where the content requires it

### Interaction language

- notice ticker
- poster lift and slight rotation
- line and underline reveals
- responsive mobile drawer
- filter changes without excessive animation
- reduced-motion fallback

### Content language

Copy is specific and task-oriented. Visitors should quickly understand:

- what is happening
- how to register
- which projects need contributors
- what resources are useful
- who currently runs the society
- who served in previous years
- what has been achieved

The result is intentionally less “perfect” and more authored: varied section structures, editorial tension, useful density, and visible institutional context.
